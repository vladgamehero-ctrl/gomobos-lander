What warehouse jobs involve

Warehouses store and distribute products for retailers, manufacturers and online stores. Typical roles include picking and packing orders, loading and unloading trucks, sorting packages, checking inventory and operating equipment such as pallet jacks or forklifts. Many positions are entry-level and provide on-the-job training.

Common shifts and schedules

Because many distribution centers run around the clock, warehouse jobs are often offered as day, evening, overnight or weekend shifts. Full-time, part-time and seasonal positions are common, and demand frequently increases ahead of busy shopping periods.

Typical requirements

Employers usually look for reliability, attention to detail and the ability to stand, walk and lift items for extended periods. Some roles require a high school diploma or equivalent, and equipment operators may need certification, which some employers help provide.

How to search locally

Job seekers often start with online job boards, company career pages and local staffing agencies. Filtering by distance, shift and job type can narrow results, and setting up alerts helps track new openings as they are posted.

Pay and benefits to compare

Hourly pay for warehouse roles varies by location, employer, shift and experience. When comparing offers, it can help to look at shift differentials, overtime rules, scheduling flexibility, benefits and opportunities to move into lead or supervisor positions.

Reading each job description carefully and confirming details with the employer can help you choose a position that fits your needs.
